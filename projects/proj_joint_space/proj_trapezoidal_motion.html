Franka Research 3 (FR3) Joint Space Motion

Time-based and velocity-based trapezoidal motion generation for smooth joint-space trajectory tracking of a robotic manipulator using ROS2.

Overview

  • Robot: Franka Research 3 (FR3) 7-DOF Manipulator
  • Framework: ROS2 Jazzy
  • Motion Profile: Trapezoidal Velocity Profile
  • Trajectory Space: Joint Space
  • Motion Generation: Time-Based and Velocity-Based
  • Controller: Joint Position Controller

In this project, a trapezoidal velocity profile is implemented for generating smooth joint-space trajectories for a robotic manipulator. The trajectory generator accepts an initial joint configuration and a desired target configuration and generates synchronized motion commands for multiple joints.

Two approaches are implemented. The time-based approach generates the trajectory for a specified total motion duration, while the velocity-based approach determines the acceleration, cruise, and deceleration durations from the specified maximum velocity, acceleration, and required joint displacement.

Multi-Joint Trajectory Synchronization

The trapezoidal velocity profile described in the accompanying article is applied independently to each joint based on its required displacement. However, when multiple joints are required to move simultaneously, each joint may require a different motion duration depending on its displacement and motion limits.

To ensure that all joints start and finish their motion at the same time, the joint trajectories are synchronized using a common motion duration. The joint requiring the largest displacement determines the overall trajectory duration, while the remaining joints scale their motion according to their respective displacement.

For a set of joint displacements $\Delta q_i = q_{f,i} - q_{0,i}$, the maximum displacement is used as the reference:

\[ \Delta q_{\max} = \max_i \left|q_{f,i} - q_{0,i}\right| \]

Each joint follows the same normalized motion profile while its trajectory magnitude is scaled according to its displacement:

\[ q_i(t) = q_{0,i} + s(t) \frac{\Delta q_i}{\Delta q_{\max}} \]

where $s(t)$ represents the scalar displacement generated by the trapezoidal profile. This allows all joints to progress through the acceleration, constant-velocity, and deceleration phases simultaneously while reaching their respective target positions at the same time.

The complete mathematical formulation of the trapezoidal velocity profile and its time-based and velocity-based forms is presented in the Trapezoidal Velocity Profile article .

Time-Based Motion Generation

In the time-based implementation, the total trajectory duration is specified by the user. The acceleration, constant-velocity, and deceleration phases are then determined from this duration while maintaining a symmetric trapezoidal velocity profile.

The current joint configuration is obtained from the ROS2 JointState topic and is stored as the initial configuration before the motion begins. The target joint configuration is then provided as the desired final configuration.

Since multiple joints may have different displacement values, the joint with the largest required displacement is used to determine the motion scale. The remaining joints follow the same normalized trajectory while being scaled according to their individual displacement.

During execution, the trajectory generator evaluates the required joint positions at every time step and publishes the resulting joint commands to the ROS2 joint position controller.

The initial configuration is captured only once at the beginning of the motion. This prevents the starting position from continuously changing while the trajectory is being executed.

Velocity-Based Motion Generation

In the velocity-based implementation, the maximum velocity and maximum acceleration are specified as the motion constraints. The required joint displacement is then used to determine the duration of the acceleration, constant-velocity, and deceleration phases.

The initial joint configuration is obtained from the current joint states before the motion begins, while the target configuration defines the required displacement for each joint.

The joint requiring the largest displacement is used to determine the overall motion duration. The remaining joints are synchronized with this trajectory by scaling their displacement relative to the maximum joint displacement.

During execution, the generated joint positions are evaluated at each sampling interval and published to the ROS2 joint position controller. This allows the manipulator to move simultaneously across multiple joints while respecting the specified velocity and acceleration constraints.

Results

The implemented trajectory generators were tested on the FR3 manipulator for multi-joint motion. Both time-based and velocity-based approaches successfully generated synchronized joint-space trajectories while maintaining the specified motion constraints.

The generated trajectories were evaluated using the joint position, velocity, and acceleration profiles. The results demonstrate the expected acceleration, constant-velocity, and deceleration phases of the trapezoidal motion profile.

Conclusion

A joint-space trajectory generation framework based on trapezoidal velocity profiles was implemented for the Franka Research 3 manipulator using ROS2. Both time-based and velocity-based approaches were developed to provide controlled and smooth joint motion.

The multi-joint synchronization approach allows all joints to start and finish their motion simultaneously while accounting for their individual displacement requirements. This provides a general framework that can be extended to manipulators with different numbers of joints.

The implementation provides direct control over the motion duration or velocity and acceleration constraints, making it suitable for controlled joint-space trajectory generation in robotic manipulation applications.
